      Method, and where it stops being measurement

      What the index is

      A composite of three published signals, equally weighted: expert-parity rate (GDPval win-or-tie, and GDPval-AA Elo above the 1000-point human baseline), autonomy (METR's 50% time horizon, log-scaled — each doubling is one step, not one hour), and deployment reach (the Anthropic Economic Index share of occupations with a quarter or more of their tasks running through the model).

      What it is not

      It is not an unemployment forecast. A task being matchable is necessary for displacement and nowhere near sufficient — cost, liability, procurement, regulation, union agreements and plain institutional inertia all sit between the two. The index measures the pressure on the door, not whether the door has opened.

      Where the placements are judgement

      Benchmark figures are cited and checkable. The crossing point assigned to each occupation is derived: it reads GDPval's occupational findings, the shape of the role's task mix, and the gate holding it, then places it on the same scale. Reasonable people would move several of them by ten points. Nobody should move them by fifty.

      The two projected stops

      2027 and 2028 are the four-month doubling continued, nothing more, and they are labelled as such. METR's own trend ran at seven months for six years before compressing; it may decompress again. Treat those two positions as the shape of an argument rather than a claim about the world.

      Why the trades sit where they do

      Every frontier benchmark on this page measures work that arrives and departs through a screen. OSWorld is the ceiling of that category. There is no equivalent, at any comparable maturity, for a fuse box in a loft or a ward round at 3am — and robotics is not on the same curve as language models.

      The honest caveat about benchmarks

      Saturation is not competence. SWE-bench Verified at 97% mostly tells you the benchmark is finished, not that engineering is. Contamination, scaffold-tuning and grader leniency all inflate scores; adversarial re-tests consistently find real success rates below headline ones. The index leans hardest on GDPval and METR precisely because they are the two hardest to game.
