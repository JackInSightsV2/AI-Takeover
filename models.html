General-purpose and specialist releases are included; restricted access is labelled on model pages. This is a sourced catalogue, not an exhaustive registry of every fine-tune. The table opens on the latest releases — scroll left to travel back to 2022. The first column and the header row stay put, and the best published figure on each benchmark is highlighted.

Most of this table is empty, and that is the finding. Labs publish the benchmarks they win. A blank cell means no comparable figure recorded or verified here, never a zero — and never a number we inferred, interpolated or estimated to fill a gap. The two evaluations with the broadest independent coverage, GDPval and METR, are also the two hardest to game, which is why the index leans on them.

How this ranks. Each model scores the mean share of the best published figure across the benchmarks it actually reports, and needs at least two published scores to rank at all — so no model can top the table on one flattering number. It still rewards selective reporting: a model that published only its two strongest results will sit higher than it deserves. Read it as a rough ordering, not a league table.

Figures are quoted as published by their maintainers, the releasing lab, or independent evaluators, at the dates shown. Where a lab published a range or revised a score after launch, the note on the model's own page says so. Every model name links to its page. † marks an extrapolation rather than a measurement.
